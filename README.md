## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
We propose a Digital Farmer Marketplace that directly connects farmers, Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs), consumers, and bulk buyers. Through this platform, farmers can sell their products directly without depending on multiple middlemen.

Explanation: The platform works as an online meeting point for sellers and buyers. Farmers and FPOs list what they have, and buyers see it and order directly. With fewer layers in between, the extra margins disappear, so the farmer earns more and the consumer pays less.

The platform will provide:

Direct buying and selling between farmers and consumers. A consumer can order straight from the farmer who grew the produce, with no agent in between.
Online product listing with price and quantity details. Farmers decide and display their own price and available stock, so buyers see clear, transparent information before ordering.
Bulk ordering facilities for shops, hotels, and supermarkets. Large buyers can place bigger, regular orders and get a steady supply, while farmers get a reliable market for larger quantities.
Logistics support for product delivery. Farmers often cannot deliver on their own. The platform arranges transport so produce reaches the buyer on time and in good condition.
AI-based demand forecasting to predict market demand. The system studies past orders and trends to estimate what will be in demand, so farmers can plan what to grow and sell.
Route optimization to reduce delivery time and transportation costs. The system finds the best delivery path for multiple orders, which saves fuel and time and keeps products fresh.

## Technical Approach
We will develop a user-friendly website or mobile application.

Explanation: Many farmers are not very comfortable with complex software, so the interface will be simple, easy to navigate and usable on a smartphone.

Frontend (what the user sees and interacts with):

HTML – builds the structure of the pages.
CSS – controls the colors, layout and design.
JavaScript – makes the pages interactive, such as search, forms and buttons.
Bootstrap – a ready-made framework that makes the site responsive, so it works well on both mobile and desktop.

Backend (the logic that runs behind the scenes):

Python – the main programming language, which is also well suited for AI and machine learning.
Flask or Django – Python frameworks that handle user requests, login, orders and communication with the database. Flask is lightweight and quick to build with, while Django has more built-in features.

Database:

MySQL – stores all the data in an organized way: user accounts, product listings, orders and delivery details.

AI Integration:

Machine Learning for demand forecasting – models trained on past sales data to predict which products will be needed, and when.
AI-based route optimization for efficient delivery – algorithms that find the shortest or cheapest route for deliveries.

Main Modules:

Farmer Registration – Farmers can create an account. They sign up with basic details, so the platform knows who the seller is and can build trust.
Product Management – Farmers can upload product details, quantity and price. They can also update stock or remove items once sold out.
Consumer Module – Consumers can search and purchase products. They can browse, compare prices, add items to a cart and place orders.
Bulk Buyer Module – Shops and hotels can place bulk orders. It supports larger quantities and repeat orders.
Logistics Module – Helps manage transportation and delivery. It tracks orders from pickup at the farm to delivery at the buyer.
AI Module – Predicts product demand and suggests better delivery routes. It connects forecasting and route planning to the rest of the system.

## Feasibility and Viability
This project is technically and economically feasible.

Explanation: The technology needed is already available and affordable, and the business model can sustain itself.

Farmers can use the platform through smartphones. Smartphones and internet access are now common even in rural areas, so farmers do not need any special equipment.
The system can be developed using affordable technologies. All the tools listed (HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Bootstrap, Python, Flask or Django, MySQL) are open-source or free, so development cost stays low.
Existing transportation services can be used for delivery. We do not need to buy our own vehicles. We can partner with local transport and delivery providers.
FPOs can help farmers join the platform. FPOs already work closely with farmers, so they can guide them in registering and using the app, which helps in building trust.
The platform can generate revenue through a small service fee. A small commission on each order keeps the platform running without burdening the farmer.
It can be expanded to different villages and cities. Once it works in one area, the same model can be repeated in other regions and with more product types.
4. Impact and Benefits

Benefits for Farmers:

Better selling prices – with the middlemen's cut removed, the farmer keeps a larger share of the price.
Direct access to customers – farmers reach buyers beyond their local market.
Increased income – better prices and more buyers together raise overall earnings.
Less dependency on middlemen – farmers get more control over when and how they sell.

Benefits for Consumers:

Fresh agricultural products – a shorter chain means produce reaches the buyer faster.
Lower purchasing prices – fewer intermediaries means fewer added margins.
Direct access to farmers – consumers know where their food comes from.
Better price transparency – prices are clearly shown, so buyers know what they are paying for.

Benefits for Society:

Reduced food wastage – faster delivery and demand forecasting mean less produce spoils unsold.
Improved rural development – higher farmer income strengthens village economies.
Better supply chain management – a more organized and efficient flow from farm to buyer.
Support for digital agriculture – it encourages farmers to adopt technology and digital tools.
